SONIC ANTI-INTIMACY

What, in Sonic Terms, Might Characterise the Opposite of Intimacy?

Sound Studies · Listening Technologies · Critical Theory

Year
2025
Context
Mediating Intimacy: Space and Intersubjectivity in Recorded Music & Sound, BA Music, University of Oxford
Length
16 pages, 3,670 words
Download PDF ↓189 KB

If sonic intimacy is sound as touch, what is its opposite? This essay argues it is neither silence nor distance, which are passive, but something designed: sound used as an active force to break connection.

I propose the term sonic anti-intimacy, a conceptual cousin of the anti-hero. It describes sound technologies that simulate closeness while producing isolation, dominance and detachment, from personal listening devices to Muzak and its algorithmic descendants, and even silence, which can work as a signal of control rather than an absence.

the research, in short

the question

What, in sonic terms, might characterise the opposite of intimacy?

the argument

Anti-intimacy is not the absence of intimacy but its active engineering: presence without connection, built into infrastructures of surveillance, capitalism and soft control.

the approach

Piekut's Actor-Network Theory, with Attali's noise, Kahn's silence and Pettman's intimacy in dialogue, applied to isolation, dominance, detachment, Muzak and silence.
